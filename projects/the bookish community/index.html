01

The Community

The Bookish Community runs a recurring "Theme of the Month" series — a monthly piece meant to visually anchor whatever the community was reading, celebrating or reflecting on that month. It was also the first real attempt at pure visual storytelling: instead of illustrating a theme literally, each month became a search for one object that could carry the idea on its own.

No layouts to follow, no brief beyond a theme name. Just a month, a feeling, and the question of what single image could hold it.

02

Finding the Object

The process was the same every month: brainstorm visual metaphors — often prompting AI for options rather than starting from a blank page — then test the strongest candidates against each other inside Photoshop until one felt inevitable rather than merely correct.

For some months the answer was immediate. For others, several ideas got built out in full before one was chosen over the rest. The community leans heavily fantasy — so the search was never just "what represents this idea," but "what represents it in a way that still feels like it belongs on this page."

January — Vision

The Crystal Ball

Vision had obvious options — a scope, a pair of glasses, spectacles. All of them read as "sight" rather than "vision." The crystal ball won because it wasn't about seeing clearly in the present; it was about seeing what hasn't happened yet, which is what the word actually meant here. The most mystical option was also the most accurate one.

February — Romeo

The Arrow and the Wreath

February's brief was romance, and the community's literary bent pointed straight at the classics rather than a generic heart. A gold arrow through a laurel wreath does the work a Valentine's heart can't — it reaches for Romeo and Juliet, and classical romance more broadly, instead of the nearest greeting-card symbol.

March — Her Story, Her Voice

The Mic in the Book

For International Women's Day, the community wanted to center women's stories being told in their own words, not just celebrated. A microphone rising out of an open book fuses the two directly — the book is the story, the mic is the fact that she's the one telling it.

April — Harry Potter's April

The Academy in the Clouds

April's theme needed no invention — it borrowed directly from Hogwarts. The idea was an academy set above the clouds, on a mountain, so the castle reads as both unmistakably Hogwarts and slightly its own place: floating just out of reach, the way the books themselves felt at that age.

May — Managerie's May

The Key

An ornate key crowned with a swirling, galaxy-like portal, a spiral staircase winding around its shaft into a scatter of tiny floating worlds. The key that opens onto more than one place — a fitting image for a "menagerie" month, even if the exact reasoning behind it has since been lost to time.

June — Agatha's June

The Study

Named for Agatha Christie, June's image needed to feel like detective fiction rather than illustrate a detective. A woman at a desk, lit only by a narrow shaft of window light and a lamp, surrounded by books in a stone room — mid-thought, mid-page, in a world that looks built rather than decorated.

03

Learning to Light It

This series was also the first time lighting was treated as a design decision rather than a finishing touch. The palette itself was difficult to work with by default — a deep, slightly muddy green, a pale, almost sickly gold, and an off-white close to cream. Left alone, all three read as dull rather than atmospheric.

Getting them to feel like a fantasy world rather than a flat color scheme meant treating each piece like scene lighting rather than flat color fills — pushing highlights until the gold actually glowed, letting the green go near-black in the shadows so the light sources had somewhere dramatic to fall. The technique came before the polish; some months show it more confidently than others, which is exactly what a first attempt at something new should look like.

04

Everyday Social

Alongside the monthly theme, the community still needed ordinary occasion posts — a Valentine's Day greeting, a new-month graphic — that didn't call for a bespoke concept, just the same visual system applied consistently. These carry the same palette and type as the themed series, but honestly: there isn't a deeper story behind them. They're the reliable, repeatable output a community account runs on between the bigger ideas.

05

The Full Year

Six months, six objects, one recurring question: what is the smallest possible image that still carries the whole idea.
