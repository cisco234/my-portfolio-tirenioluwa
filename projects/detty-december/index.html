DETTY DECEMBER FEST — BRAND IDENTITY SYSTEM

A festival identity built from three words and worked all the way through to merch.

Role Brand Identity & Graphic Design
Scope Concept, colour system, logo, type, applications
Status Concept project

01

The Brief

DETTY DECEMBER FEST is Lagos's own December — the run of concerts, block parties and homecomings that take over the city every year. The identity needed to carry that energy across a full campaign: a main flyer, individual artist posters, merch, and everything in between, without turning into a set of unrelated graphics that happened to share a logo.

Before touching colour or type, the brief got reduced to keywords. Running the brief through a synonym pass surfaced the words that actually mattered: energy, vibrancy, diversity, and a specific kind of cultural connection — not generic "party" energy, but a Lagos one.

Youthful Energetic Bold Expression Diversity Vibrant Cultural Connectivity

02

Direction & Mood

The moodboard pulled from two places at once: editorial Afrofuturist portraiture with hard colour blocking, and straight fashion photography — sunglasses, statement accessories, clean product shots. The point wasn't to copy either register but to find where they overlap: bold, graphic, confident, still rooted in the culture rather than abstracted from it.

Reference pulls that set the tone before any design decisions were made: bold colour blocking, confident portraiture, product-level polish.

03

The Colour System

The first pass at the palette used a pink in place of red. It read closer to a generic party flyer than to anything specifically Nigerian. Swapping that pink for a deep red — alongside gold, black, dark green and an off-white — pulled the palette toward the Nigerian flag without spelling it out literally, and gave the identity a set of colours that could hold up as flat blocks, not just gradients and photo overlays.

04

The Mark

The logo went through three iterations. The first two leaned on a sunburst — flame-like rays radiating off a half-circle — sitting above a stacked wordmark, but the burst competed with the type at small sizes and didn't survive being shrunk onto a poster corner or a t-shirt sleeve. The final version keeps a scaled-down version of that same burst as an accent mark beside the wordmark, rather than as the dominant shape, so "DETTY DECEMBER" carries the logo at any size.

First iteration, second iteration, final iteration. The burst shrinks from the whole idea to a supporting accent.

The final mark, checked on both dark and light ground before it went anywhere near a poster.

05

Typography

Gulfs Display carries the title role — chunky, a little irregular, expressive enough to match the festival's energy without tipping into novelty. Brice SemiExpanded sits underneath it as the secondary face, doing the quieter job of staying legible at body-copy sizes: dates, venue details, the fine print a flyer still has to communicate clearly even while the headline is shouting.

Gulfs Display, primary — Brice SemiExpanded, secondary.

06

Texture, Pattern & Imagery

Flat colour and clean type risked feeling too corporate for a street festival, so every surface got a layer of grain and a warm vintage colour filter — a nod to the Fela Kuti era that "Detty December" as a phrase already gestures toward. An Ankara-derived geometric pattern became the system's texture signature, repeated across merch and collateral as a recognisable fill rather than a one-off decoration.

Grain texture, retro filter, Ankara pattern — the three finishing layers applied across the system.

07

Campaign Flyers

The main flyer sets the template every artist poster follows: the theme line stacked in three colours, event details locked to the bottom edge, the Ankara border running top and bottom. Individual performer posters swap in a portrait and drop the stacked theme line down to a corner tag, so the series reads as one campaign even as the hero image changes night to night.

Main campaign flyer — theme, venue and time all locked to the same grid.

Individual artist posters — Burna Boy, Simi, Odumodublvck — carrying the same system with a new hero portrait each time.

08

Merch & Applications

The real test of a flat identity is what happens once it leaves the poster and has to sit on fabric. The Ankara pattern became a full all-over print for one t-shirt run, while a second run uses it sparingly as a sleeve and shoulder accent so the wordmark stays the focal point. Tote bags carry the same split: one fully patterned, one restrained to the flame mark and wordmark on natural canvas.

All-over Ankara pattern print, on white and black.

Wordmark-led version, pattern pulled back to a sleeve and shoulder accent.

Patterned tote, left — restrained canvas tote with the flame mark, right.

09

The System, Not the Poster

None of these pieces are interchangeable, and they weren't meant to be. What carries across a flyer, a t-shirt sleeve and a tote bag is the same small set of decisions — the red-gold-green-black palette, the Ankara pattern as a repeatable fill, Gulfs Display doing the shouting while Brice SemiExpanded does the reading. A system built once, applied everywhere it was needed.

"The identity was never the flyer. It was the small set of rules that let every flyer, shirt and bag still read as one night."
